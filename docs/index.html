A SuuntoPlus app for navigators

Your next waypoint,
wherever you are.

Navigator turns a Suunto watch with GPS and SuuntoPlus into a navigation instrument on your wrist. Bearing, distance and time to every waypoint of your route, on the line or well away from it. In the air, at sea or on foot.

Prepare your routes How to use it

What it brings

Guidance that follows you

Few routes are followed on the exact line. Wind or current sets you off it, a cloud, a headland or a closed path sends you around. Give Navigator your route and it knows every waypoint exactly, so the bearing and distance to the next one stay on the screen wherever you are.

A navigator's numbers

Nautical miles and knots, the units of the air and the sea. Time to run and time of arrival, for the next waypoint and for the end of the route. Your position in degrees, minutes and seconds, ready to read out on the radio.

One glance to stay on course

A marker on the bezel shows where the waypoint is against your track over the ground. Keep it at the top and you are on your way. Green within ten degrees, red beyond.

Built on the watch's navigation

Start your route from the watch's own Navigation menu, as usual. Navigator reads the watch's distance to the next waypoint, accurate to about a meter, and extends it to the whole route, on the line and off it.

How to use it

  1. Prepare, if you wish

    Optional, and worth it. Without it, Navigator relies on the watch's own navigation and works out each waypoint as you head for it along the route. With your routes prepared, every waypoint is exact from the start, on the line or off it. Export your route as GPX from your planner and drop it on the converter, with a few others if you like. The conversion runs in your browser, and the file never leaves your machine.

  2. Load

    Import your GPX into the Suunto app and sync, then install Navigator, with the routes you prepared if any, and add it as a data screen to a GPS sport mode.

  3. Go

    Start the activity and start navigating your route from the watch's Navigation menu. Navigator proposes the route nearest to you and shows its name. Set off, and press lap at each waypoint to keep your leg times.

Three buttons, no menu

Down, click
A lap, as always. Your leg times stay in the activity.
Down, hold
Shows the route you follow. While its name is up, click for the next route, nearest first, hold for the one before. The last choice, --, follows the watch's navigation alone.
Up, click
Skip to the next waypoint, for a leg you cut short.
Up, hold
Back to the waypoint before, to go through it again.

Navigator follows the leg you are nearest to, so a skipped waypoint or a change of route is picked up on its own. The buttons are there when you know better.

Good to know

  • Navigator is a navigation aid. It does not replace your chart, your compass or your judgment, and it is not certified for anything.
  • It has been used in the air and on foot. It has not been tried at sea yet.
  • It has been tested on a Suunto Ocean, and its screen is laid out for that watch's round 466 pixel display. On another SuuntoPlus watch the screen may need adjusting.
  • Where your route crosses itself, the waypoint name can switch to the other leg for a few seconds as you pass over the crossing.
  • Bearings are true, not magnetic. The difference is the magnetic variation where you are.
  • For now Navigator is installed from its source, with the SuuntoPlus Editor for Visual Studio Code. The repository explains how.
